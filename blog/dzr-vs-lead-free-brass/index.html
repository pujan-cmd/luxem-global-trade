DZR Brass vs Lead-Free Brass: Which Do You Need for Water Fittings?

1 Oct 20262 min readLuxem Global Trade export desk
← All articles

Buyers of plumbing components regularly ask for "DZR or lead-free brass" as though they were alternatives. They are two separate properties, and the right choice depends on where the product will be used.

What DZR brass does

Dezincification is selective corrosion: in certain water conditions, zinc is removed from the alloy and a porous, weak copper structure remains. DZR (dezincification-resistant) brass, such as CW602N, adds a small amount of arsenic and relies on a controlled microstructure to resist this. Resistance is normally demonstrated with a laboratory test such as ISO 6509.

What lead-free means

Lead-free brass limits the lead content, mainly for products that contact drinking water. Definitions differ by market. In the United States, for example, "lead free" plumbing is defined by a weighted-average lead content of no more than 0.25% on wetted surfaces. Europe is moving toward harmonised approved-material lists for products in contact with drinking water.

The point that is often missed

Traditional DZR brasses are still leaded alloys — CW602N typically contains roughly 2% lead — so DZR does not automatically mean lead-free. Some lead-free silicon brasses also show good dezincification resistance, but this should be verified against the specific alloy and test standard you require.

Which should you specify?

Your situationTypically specify
Aggressive water, no lead restrictionDZR brass (CW602N)
Drinking-water product in a lead-restricted marketLead-free brass (e.g. CW724R)
Both requirements applyLead-free alloy verified for dezincification resistance
Gas, compressed air or general hardwareCW617N or CW614N

Key takeaways

  • DZR resists dezincification corrosion; lead-free limits lead content. They are not the same thing.
  • Standard DZR brasses usually still contain lead, so they may not meet lead-free rules.
  • State the market and the test standard you need when you request a quotation.
