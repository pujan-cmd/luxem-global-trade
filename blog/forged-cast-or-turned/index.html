Forged, Cast or Turned? Choosing the Right Brass Manufacturing Process

30 Jul 20262 min readLuxem Global Trade export desk
← All articles

Many brass parts can be made in more than one way. The best choice depends on volume, shape, strength requirements and tolerance. Understanding the trade-offs helps you ask the right question when you request a quote.

ProcessBest forStrengthsWatch-outs
Turning (CNC / multi-spindle)Round parts, threads, small precision componentsTight tolerances, excellent finish, no tooling costMaterial waste from bar; slower for large bodies
Hot forgingValve bodies, fittings, flangesDense grain, pressure-tight, efficient material useDie cost; needs machining for threads and faces
Casting (sand / gravity die)Complex shapes, low to medium volumesLower tooling cost, intricate geometryPossible porosity; rougher surface

Rules of thumb

  • If the part holds pressure — water, gas, hydraulic — forging is generally preferred over casting.
  • If volumes are small or the shape is mostly cylindrical, turning from bar avoids tooling cost altogether.
  • If volumes are high and the shape is closer to the final form, forging then machining usually gives the lowest piece price.
  • If the geometry is intricate and pressure is not critical, casting may be the economical route.

Many parts use more than one process

A valve body is typically forged, then machined to cut threads and sealing faces, then plated. Describing the function of the part in your RFQ lets us recommend the most sensible route.

Key takeaways

  • Turning gives the best precision and finish for small parts, with more material waste.
  • Forging gives dense, pressure-tight parts and efficient material use at higher volumes.
  • Casting suits complex shapes and lower tooling cost, with more attention needed on porosity.
