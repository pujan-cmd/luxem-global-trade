Material Test Certificates for Brass: What EN 10204 3.1 Means

27 Aug 20262 min readLuxem Global Trade export desk
← All articles

A material test certificate (MTC) links the parts you receive to the material they were made from. EN 10204 defines the main types, and the differences matter when your customer or your quality system requires traceability.

TypeWhat it is
2.1Declaration of compliance with the order — no test results
2.2Test report — results from non-specific testing of the manufacturer's production
3.1Inspection certificate — specific tests on the delivered lot, validated by the manufacturer's authorised inspection representative who is independent of production
3.2As 3.1, and also validated by the purchaser's representative or an independent third party

What a brass certificate typically shows

  • Alloy designation and the standard it conforms to
  • Chemical composition of the lot (copper, zinc, lead and other elements)
  • Mechanical properties where specified, such as tensile strength or hardness
  • Lot or heat number for traceability, and the manufacturer's details

Practical tips

Certificates belong to a material lot, so they must be requested when the material is purchased. Ask for the type you need at the time you place the order — it is difficult to produce a 3.1 certificate retrospectively. If you need a 3.2 certificate, tell your supplier early so that inspection can be arranged.

Key takeaways

  • A 3.1 certificate reports test results for the delivered lot, validated by the manufacturer's inspection representative.
  • Request the certificate type in your purchase order, before production.
  • The certificate should be traceable to the lot number marked on the material.
