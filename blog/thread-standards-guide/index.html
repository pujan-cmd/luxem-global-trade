BSP, NPT and Metric Threads: A Buyer's Guide for Brass Fittings

24 Sept 20262 min readLuxem Global Trade export desk
← All articles

A fitting that "almost" screws together is a fitting that will eventually leak. Thread standards that look similar can differ in flank angle, pitch or form, and brass — a relatively soft metal — will be damaged if they are forced together. These are the families we are asked for most often.

ThreadStandardFormTypical use
BSPP (G)ISO 228-1Parallel, 55°Seals with a washer or O-ring; Europe, Asia, Middle East
BSPT (R / Rc)ISO 7-1Taper, 55°Seals on the thread with sealant
NPTASME B1.20.1Taper, 60°North American pipe threads
Metric (M)ISO 261 / 262Parallel, 60°Sensors, glands, fasteners
UNC / UNFASME B1.1Parallel, 60°Imperial fasteners and fittings

Parallel vs taper

Parallel threads do not seal by themselves; a bonded washer, O-ring or flat face provides the seal. Taper threads wedge together and seal on the thread flanks, usually with PTFE tape or sealant. Specifying the wrong type is a design error, not a manufacturing one.

Do not mix BSPT and NPT

Some sizes share the same threads per inch, which makes the two look interchangeable. The flank angles differ (55° versus 60°), so the joint may start to engage but will not seal reliably and can damage both parts.

What to put on the drawing

  • Thread standard and designation, e.g. "G 1/2 – ISO 228-1, class A"
  • Internal or external thread, and the usable length
  • Tolerance class or gauge requirement (go / no-go)
  • Finish or plating that will affect thread dimensions

We gauge threads with plug and ring gauges during production and can supply a gauge record on request. Browse our fittings and inserts and fasteners for standard thread options.

Key takeaways

  • BSP threads have a 55° flank angle; NPT and metric threads have 60°.
  • Parallel threads seal with a washer or O-ring; taper threads seal on the thread itself.
  • Always state the thread standard, size, gender and tolerance on the drawing.
